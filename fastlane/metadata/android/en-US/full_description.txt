Sigil is a phone client for the sQUIC exchange: voice calls and end-to-end
encrypted messaging with the people, groups and channels of an account whose
key lives on your own machine.

A phone is a device of the account, not the account. Start a new account on
the phone itself, or pair it from a desktop Sigil in two strings. Either way
there is no password, no phone number and no e-mail address, and the phone
holds a key of its own, sealed by the Android key store. Remove the device
from the account and it is gone.

Features

* Voice calls, one to one and in rooms, that ring on a locked screen
* Direct messages, groups and public channels; replies, reactions, edits,
  files and images
* Notifications composed on the phone from messages only it can decrypt,
  under your setting of how much they say
* Two ways to hear about a call or a message while the app is closed: a
  UnifiedPush distributor of your choosing (ntfy and others), or "Stay
  reachable", which keeps the connection open from the phone itself and
  needs nothing installed
* Nothing sent to the developer: no analytics, no crash reporting, no
  advertising, and the phone's contacts are never read

No Google services

Sigil contains no Firebase, no Google Play Services and no tracking library
of any kind. Push, if you want it, goes through UnifiedPush and a
distributor you chose; without one the app can stay reachable on its own.

What you need

An account on an exchange. Run your own, or use one that is open to
newcomers -- the app suggests trunk.exchange, where a new account may claim
a name and is put into a public channel so there is somebody to talk to.

What the exchange can and cannot see

Messages, files and call audio are encrypted between the devices that hold
the keys; the exchange stores ciphertext and cannot read any of it. It does
know which device connected and when, and which conversations your account
is in -- membership is what it enforces on, so it is held in the clear.
"The exchange cannot read your messages" is true; it is not the same
sentence as "the exchange does not know who you talk to".
